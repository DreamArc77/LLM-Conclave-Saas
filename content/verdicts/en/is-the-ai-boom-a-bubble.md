## Council verdict
The council concludes that in 2026, the AI boom represents a financial and capital-allocation bubble rather than an underlying technological failure. A substantial valuation correction is likely for speculative, unprofitable startups, superficial software wrappers, and foundation model providers experiencing margin compression due to an unsustainable CapEx-to-monetization mismatch. However, real enterprise adoption, declining inference costs, and resilient mega-cap balance sheets will prevent a total sector collapse. Investors and enterprises should underweight speculative AI assets and concentrate exposure on cash-flow-positive infrastructure and durable application leaders. Overall confidence: 7.5/10.

## Where the council agreed
* **Financial versus technological reality:** AI utility and enterprise productivity gains are permanent; the bubble is strictly confined to asset pricing, circular vendor financing, and overextended capital expenditure.
* **CapEx-to-revenue divergence:** Hundreds of billions of dollars spent on compute, energy, and data centers have outpaced near-term, high-margin end-user monetization.
* **Imminent shakeout in speculative assets:** Unprofitable startups, commoditized foundation models, and speculative derivatives face an inevitable, sharp valuation reset.
* **Balance sheet resilience:** Unlike the 2000 dot-com crash, the core buildout is heavily underwritten by cash-generative technology incumbents, preventing a broader liquidity catastrophe.

## Where it disagreed
* **Severity and reach of the correction:** Gemini and DeepSeek view circular financing and valuation excesses as systemic pressures guaranteeing a sharp market-wide repricing within 12–24 months, whereas Luna considers them contained pockets of excess that may not precipitate a severe sector-wide downturn.
* **Moat durability:** Gemini emphasizes that rapid advances in open-weight models have already commoditized raw intelligence and destroyed pricing power, while Luna anticipates that sustained demand and falling inference costs can preserve margins for high-utility players.

## Risks and caveats
* **Timing uncertainty:** Diagnosing cyclical peaks is inherently speculative; ongoing enterprise adoption and private capital reserves could prolong elevated valuations longer than macroeconomic fundamentals suggest.
* **Enterprise friction and integration debt:** Persistent operational hurdles—such as data governance, reliability risks, and legacy integration costs—may depress realized productivity, worsening the ROI gap before infrastructure investments mature.
