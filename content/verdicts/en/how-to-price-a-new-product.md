## Council verdict
To price a product without direct market comparables, anchor the price to the quantifiable economic value generated by replacing the customer’s status quo, treating delivery costs strictly as a viability floor. Launch with a defensible provisional price validated through paid pilots or controlled offers where customers commit real capital rather than hypothetical feedback. Adjust pricing iteratively across defined segments as conversion, retention, and margin data accumulate. Overall confidence level: 8.5/10.

## Where the council agreed
* **The status quo is the true comparable:** True "no comparables" situations rarely exist; the buyer’s current cost of friction, manual labor, or inaction provides the baseline for Economic Value to Customer (EVC).
* **Cost serves only as a floor:** Cost-plus pricing is rejected for novel products because it ignores utility; costs exist solely to verify unit-economic viability.
* **Revealed preference over stated preference:** Paid pilots and contractual commitments yield dependable willingness-to-pay data, whereas hypothetical surveys (e.g., Van Westendorp) are unreliable for novel paradigms.
* **Empirical iteration:** Initial pricing must be treated as a provisional hypothesis that is refined via early conversion, win-loss, and churn metrics.

## Where it disagreed
* **Launch price posture (Skimming vs. Defensible entry):** Gemini advocated launching high to exploit price anchoring and leave room for discounting; Luna and DeepSeek argued that defaulting to skimming can suppress early adoption, starve the company of learning, and invite copycat competitors.
* **Standardized value-capture rules:** Gemini proposed standardizing on capturing 10% to 30% of quantified economic value, whereas Luna and DeepSeek rejected rigid formulas, arguing that value is often qualitative or difficult to isolate initially.

## Risks and caveats
* **Anchoring asymmetry:** Setting a price too low makes future increases difficult to justify, while pricing too high can kill early-stage momentum and conceal product-market fit.
* **Underestimating switching inertia:** Economic value calculations often fail in practice because they discount the hidden organizational costs, risk aversion, and effort required for a customer to abandon their status quo.
