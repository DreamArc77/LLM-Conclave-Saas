## Council verdict
A capital-constrained startup should default to B2B first unless the founding team possesses an unfair, zero-cost consumer distribution advantage or innate product virality. B2B provides concentrated revenue, higher contract values, and direct outbound access to buyers driven by clear ROI rather than fickle consumer psychology. However, founders must prioritize validation velocity over rigid labels by choosing the segment where they have the fastest path to budget-holders. Overall confidence: 8/10.

## Where the council agreed
* **B2B as the baseline default:** Higher contract values allow a startup to validate willingness to pay and sustain runway with a handful of customers, avoiding the massive initial scale B2C demands.
* **Tractable customer validation:** Finding and interviewing a targeted group of business stakeholders yields clearer, higher-signal product feedback than diagnosing noisy metrics and churn in consumer cohorts.
* **The high bar for B2C first:** B2C is only viable as an initial path if the product features organic virality, low customer acquisition costs (CAC), or access to an existing audience; otherwise, paid acquisition will exhaust capital.

## Where it disagreed
* **Speed to revenue within B2B:** While initial inputs framed B2B as universally faster to monetize, the council divided on execution risk. Gemini argued for explicitly restricting the B2B default to SMBs or prosumers with rapid credit-card purchasing, whereas Luna and DeepSeek cautioned that enterprise procurement, compliance, and 6–12 month sales cycles can make B2B far slower and more fatal to runway than consumer launches.

## Risks and caveats
* **The enterprise sales trap:** Targeting mid-market or enterprise B2B without sufficient capital risks death by pilot, lengthy procurement, and bespoke feature requests before closing revenue.
* **Consumer monetization desert:** Choosing B2C without an established distribution engine often leads to high vanity engagement paired with near-zero willingness to pay and unsustainable churn.
