## Council verdict
The council recommends that you **hold** the property by default, unless a rigorous review of your numbers reveals an unacceptably low return on equity or persistent negative cash flow. You should not sell purely in an attempt to time market cycles, as hefty transaction costs and tax burdens will immediately erode your equity. Selling is justified primarily if the asset severely underperforms lower-hassle alternatives, you require the capital for an immediate high-conviction need, or the property has become an unsustainable management burden.
**Overall Confidence Level: 5/10** (a definitive recommendation requires your specific mortgage rate, equity position, tax bracket, and net operating income).

## Where the council agreed
* **Avoid market timing:** Decisions should be driven by property-level fundamentals and return on capital, not by attempts to predict local or national home-price peaks.
* **Significant exit friction:** Selling triggers substantial transaction costs—including broker fees, capital gains taxes, and depreciation recapture—which significantly discount net proceeds unless deferred via a 1031 exchange.
* **Return on Equity (ROE) as the primary test:** The decision hinges on comparing your after-expense, after-tax yield on current equity against realistic alternative investments (such as index funds or Treasuries).
* **Operational burden matters:** If managing vacancies, repairs, and tenants no longer aligns with your risk tolerance or lifestyle, exiting is rational even in a favorable market.

## Where it disagreed
* **Significance of low-rate debt:** Gemini emphasized a low fixed mortgage rate as an irreplaceable financial asset that strongly favors holding, whereas Luna and DeepSeek countered that cheap debt should never anchor an investor to a property generating sub-par returns on trapped equity.

## Risks and caveats
* **Unanticipated tax liabilities:** Failure to model capital gains and depreciation recapture before listing can result in unexpectedly low net cash proceeds upon closing.
* **Operational and liquidity shocks:** Choosing to hold requires sufficient cash reserves to withstand tenant default, prolonged vacancy, and major capital expenditures that can quickly erase thin cash flows.
