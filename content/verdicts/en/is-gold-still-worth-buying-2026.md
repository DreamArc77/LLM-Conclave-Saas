## Council verdict
Gold remains worth buying in 2026, but exclusively as defensive portfolio insurance and a macroeconomic hedge rather than an engine for aggressive growth. Structural tailwinds—principally non-Western central bank reserve diversification and sovereign debt constraints—justify maintaining a modest position. However, after substantial multi-year price gains, investors should avoid aggressive lump-sum bets and instead accumulate gradually on market dips. Overall confidence: 7/10.

## Where the council agreed
* **Role as portfolio insurance:** Gold should be treated strictly as a counterparty-free diversifier and wealth-preservation tool against geopolitical fragmentation and fiscal risks, not as an income-generating or speculative asset.
* **Macroeconomic tailwinds:** Sustained central bank demand to de-risk foreign reserves and high sovereign debt burdens that constrain long-term real yields provide enduring structural support.
* **Prudent execution:** Acquisition should occur incrementally via dollar-cost averaging on pullbacks, using low-cost ETFs or physical holdings without leverage or capital needed for short-term liquidity.

## Where it disagreed
* **Reliability of the price floor:** Gemini argued that central bank demand creates a dependable, price-insensitive floor; conversely, Luna and DeepSeek warned that much of this demand is already priced in, leaving gold vulnerable to extended drawdowns.
* **Universal allocation sizing:** DeepSeek and Gemini recommended a standard 5–10% portfolio allocation, whereas Luna argued against any fixed universal target, emphasizing that sizing must depend entirely on individual investor horizon, entry price, and existing asset mix.

## Risks and caveats
* **Yield opportunity cost:** Gold generates no income; if real interest rates remain resilient or the US dollar strengthens durably, holding non-yielding gold entails a substantial opportunity cost.
* **Extended stagnation risk:** Following extensive rallies, valuations are elevated, creating the risk of multi-year periods of negative or stagnant real returns similar to historical post-peak cycles.
