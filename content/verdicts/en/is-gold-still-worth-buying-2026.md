## Council verdict
Yes, gold remains worth buying in 2026, primarily as a defensive portfolio hedge and diversifier rather than a vehicle for rapid capital appreciation. The council broadly recommends a modest allocation of 5–10%, accumulated gradually through dollar-cost averaging to mitigate the risk of buying at cycle highs. Investors should treat the metal as insurance against fiscal dominance, geopolitical friction, and currency debasement. Overall confidence: 7/10.

## Where the council agreed
* **Portfolio role:** Gold should function as non-correlated insurance and a capital-preservation tool (targeted at 5–10% of a portfolio), not as an income-generating or speculative asset.
* **Execution method:** Given elevated valuations after multi-year rallies, investors should avoid leverage and lump-sum purchases in favor of systematic dollar-cost averaging or buying on dips.
* **Structural demand drivers:** Multipolar geopolitical friction, persistent sovereign debt expansion, and sustained central-bank diversification away from the U.S. dollar provide a durable baseline of demand.

## Where it disagreed
* **Performance outlook:** Gemini contends that fiscal constraints on central banks will allow gold to actively outperform sovereign fixed income. Conversely, Luna and DeepSeek maintain that structural drivers are already priced in, viewing gold strictly as low-yielding, passive insurance with historically mediocre long-term real returns.

## Risks and caveats
* **Macro headwinds:** Gold produces no cash yield; sustained high real interest rates or renewed U.S. dollar strength could lead to significant underperformance and sharp drawdowns.
* **Valuation and timing risk:** Buying after a prolonged rally leaves capital vulnerable to cyclical corrections or multi-year stagnation if inflation normalizes faster than anticipated.
