## Council verdict
A student should prioritize a lightweight ultraportable (13–14 inches) featuring 8+ hours of real-world battery life, durable build quality, and a comfortable keyboard. The sweet-spot hardware configuration for four-year longevity is a modern mid-range processor (Intel Core i5/5, AMD Ryzen 5, or Apple M-series), 16 GB of RAM, and a 512 GB SSD. Crucially, students must verify their academic department’s software requirements before committing to an operating system or buying expensive dedicated graphics. Overall confidence is rated at 9/10.

## Where the council agreed
* **Mobility and endurance:** Real-world all-day battery life (8+ hours) and a lightweight chassis are non-negotiable for commuting and taking notes away from wall outlets.
* **Ergonomics over raw power:** Keyboards, trackpads, and screen quality matter far more for daily academic work than dedicated gaming GPUs.
* **Core specification baseline:** A modern mid-range processor combined with a 512 GB SSD offers the best balance of speed, storage, and value.
* **Curriculum-first operating system:** Departmental software requirements must dictate whether to purchase macOS or Windows, as certain STEM or business tools are platform-exclusive.

## Where it disagreed
* **Minimum RAM requirement:** Gemini insisted on 16 GB as a hard requirement for four-year multitasking longevity, whereas DeepSeek and Luna argued 8 GB remains acceptable for humanities students on tight budgets, freeing funds for better build quality.
* **Screen size limits:** Gemini recommended strictly 13–14 inches to fit cramped lecture hall desks, while DeepSeek accepted sizes up to 15 inches.

## Risks and caveats
* **Operating system incompatibility:** Purchasing a MacBook without verifying course syllabi risks running into Windows-only proprietary software, particularly in engineering, architecture, or finance.
* **The "gaming laptop" trap:** Buying machines with power-hungry dedicated graphics often results in heavy chassis, poor battery endurance, and bulky chargers unsuited for classroom use.
* **False economy of ultra-budget models:** Sub-$400 new machines often cut corners on hinges, keyboard durability, and long-term performance; certified refurbished business-class models frequently offer superior reliability at the same price.

### Round 1 — Independent answers

#### GPT-6 Luna

Look for a reliable, lightweight laptop with all-day battery life, a comfortable keyboard, and enough memory and storage for several years of schoolwork—ideally 16 GB RAM and a 512 GB SSD if your budget allows. Check your school’s software requirements first; specialized courses may need Windows or a more powerful machine. These matter most because students carry laptops daily, depend on them for long classes, and benefit from avoiding slowdowns or early replacement. Don’t overspend on gaming-level performance unless your coursework requires it. Confidence: 9/10.

#### DeepSeek V4.1 Flash

Buy a reliable, lightweight 14–15″ laptop with 16GB RAM, 512GB SSD, a current mid-range Ryzen 5 or Intel i5, and 8+ hours of battery, ideally under $800. Skip dedicated graphics unless your major requires it. Decisive reasons: it will handle four years of writing, research, video calls, and light coding without slowing down; long battery life and low weight mean you actually carry it to class; and mid-range specs plus a good warranty beat a cheap, fragile machine or an overpowered gaming laptop. Prioritize screen quality and keyboard comfort too. Confidence: 8/10.

#### Gemini 3.8 Flash

A student should prioritize an ultraportable laptop featuring at least 16GB of RAM, a 512GB SSD, a modern mid-range processor (Apple M-series, AMD Ryzen 5, or Intel Core 5), and 8+ hours of real-world battery life. A MacBook Air or an Asus Zenbook is typically the sweet spot.

Decisive reasons:
1. Portability and Endurance: Students constantly commute and study away from outlets; a lightweight chassis (under 3.3 lbs) and genuine all-day battery are non-negotiable.
2. 16GB RAM for Longevity: Multitasking with heavy browser tabs, documents, and background software quickly exhausts 8GB; 16GB ensures the machine lasts a full four-year degree without slowing down.
3. Usability/Build: A durable chassis, comfortable keyboard, and reliable trackpad matter far more for coursework than dedicated gaming graphics.

Confidence: 9/10.
