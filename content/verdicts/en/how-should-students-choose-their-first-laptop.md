## Council verdict
For a first student laptop, choose a lightweight 13-to-14-inch machine equipped with 16 GB of RAM, a 512 GB SSD, and at least 8 to 10 hours of real-world battery life. Students should prioritize chassis durability, typing comfort, and display usability over raw processing power or dedicated graphics cards, which are unnecessary for most non-engineering disciplines. Before buying, verify that the operating system meets all specific course requirements and ensure the purchase includes a dependable warranty. Overall confidence: 9/10.

## Where the council agreed
* **Core hardware baseline:** 16 GB of RAM and a 512 GB SSD represent the non-negotiable standard to ensure multitasking performance and multi-year longevity.
* **Mobility and endurance:** A 13–14-inch form factor under 1.5 kg (around 3 pounds) with 8+ hours of genuine battery life is essential for moving between lectures without hunting for power outlets.
* **Ergonomics over raw power:** Typing comfort, a dependable trackpad, and a robust physical build are far more impactful for day-to-day coursework than high-end CPU benchmarks.
* **Skip dedicated GPUs:** Dedicated graphics cards add weight, drain battery life, and raise costs unnecessarily, unless the student is specifically enrolled in 3D modeling, game development, or demanding engineering programs.

## Where it disagreed
* **Refurbished enterprise vs. new consumer:** Panelists debated whether students should buy refurbished business-class ultrabooks (e.g., Lenovo ThinkPad, Dell Latitude) or Apple Silicon for superior build quality, versus buying new machines. Opposing views highlighted that refurbished units present risks regarding degraded battery health, limited warranties, and lack of immediate support for a first-time buyer.

## Risks and caveats
* **Software incompatibility:** Failing to consult departmental software guidelines before choosing between macOS and Windows can lead to costly disruptions if specialized academic software is restricted to a specific platform.
* **Overlooked display and port specs:** Cheaper models frequently compromise on display brightness (under 300 nits can cause eye strain under campus lighting) and physical port variety, which may require carrying awkward dongles.
* **Hidden costs of used hardware:** Buying refurbished hardware without a verified return window or battery health guarantee risks immediate out-of-pocket battery replacement costs.
