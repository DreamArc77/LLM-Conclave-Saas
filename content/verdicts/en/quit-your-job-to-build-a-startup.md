## Council verdict
Do not quit your job yet. The council unanimously recommends remaining employed until you have concrete validation of customer demand, demonstrated willingness to pay, and secured adequate personal savings. Quitting prematurely converts a low-cost experiment into an acute financial survival crisis, which compromises founder judgment. You should resign only when customer traction makes staying at your current job the primary bottleneck to company growth, or when the venture is structurally impossible to pursue part-time. 
Overall confidence level: 8/10.

## Where the council agreed
* **Demand precedes full-time commitment:** Market risk—building a product nobody wants—dwarfs execution risk; customer discovery and interest must be verified before sacrificing your primary income.
* **Financial runway protects judgment:** Your current salary acts as non-dilutive funding; running out of cash induces panic that leads to predatory investment terms, bad customer selection, and flawed product pivots.
* **Defined exit criteria:** Resigning should be triggered by explicit metrics, specifically paying customers or binding commitments alongside a baseline personal runway of roughly 12 months.

## Where it disagreed
* **Feasibility of part-time validation:** Gemini initially favored building on nights and weekends, whereas DeepSeek and GPT-6 Luna argued that side-project validation is structurally unviable for deep tech, hardware, regulated sectors, or enterprise sales requiring full-time credibility.
* **IP and contractual constraints:** Gemini emphasized that moonlighting poses severe legal hazards due to employer intellectual property assignment clauses, a structural barrier the other models treated as secondary to commercial validation.

## Risks and caveats
* **Legal ownership forfeiture:** Working on a side business while bound by employment agreements or using employer resources can grant your employer legal ownership of your startup’s IP.
* **Analysis paralysis and burnout:** Over-relying on part-time validation can result in chronic exhaustion, slow execution in competitive windows, or permanently postponing launch under the guise of needing more data.
